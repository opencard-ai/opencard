# Capital One Quicksilver vs Wells Fargo Active Cash

If you want one card that pays the same rate on everything, two of the most common picks are:

- [Capital One Quicksilver Cash Rewards](/en/cards/capital-one-quicksilver) — **1.5% cash back** on every purchase
- [Wells Fargo Active Cash](/en/cards/wells-fargo-active-cash) — **2% cash rewards** on purchases

Neither card has an annual fee, and neither makes you track categories. On paper, 2% beats 1.5%, so Active Cash looks like the easy win. The catch is foreign transactions: Quicksilver charges none, while Active Cash charges 3%. That one line decides the comparison for a lot of people.

Facts below come from Capital One's and Wells Fargo's card pages and our card records (checked November 2, 2026). Welcome offers and intro APRs change often, so they aren't quoted here — check the issuer page before you apply.

## Side by side

| | Capital One Quicksilver | Wells Fargo Active Cash |
|---|---|---|
| Annual fee | $0 | $0 |
| Everyday rate | Unlimited 1.5% cash back | Unlimited 2% cash rewards |
| Foreign transaction fee | None | 3% |
| Extra earn | 5% on hotels, vacation rentals and rental cars booked through Capital One Travel | — |
| Categories to track | None | None |
| Best for | Travelers abroad, or anyone who buys from overseas merchants | Spending that stays in U.S. dollars |

## The domestic math: Active Cash wins

For purchases in U.S. dollars, the comparison is simple. The extra 0.5 percentage point is worth:

- **$5** for every $1,000 you spend
- **$50** a year at $10,000 of spending
- **$100** a year at $20,000 of spending

There's no cap on either card's flat rate, so that gap keeps growing with spend. If all of your purchases are domestic and you only want one card, Active Cash earns more.

## The foreign math: Quicksilver wins, by a lot

A 3% foreign transaction fee is larger than any flat cash back rate. On Active Cash, each $1,000 charged in a foreign currency (or processed by a foreign bank) earns $20 in rewards but costs $30 in fees — a **net loss of $10**. On Quicksilver, the same $1,000 earns **$15** with no fee.

So every $1,000 of foreign spending swings **$25** in Quicksilver's favor, while every $1,000 of domestic spending gives Active Cash only $5.

### Break-even point

Put those together and the rule of thumb is roughly 1 to 5:

- If your foreign spending is **less than about one-fifth of your domestic spending** (about 17% of the total), Active Cash comes out ahead.
- If it's **more than that**, Quicksilver comes out ahead.

Example: $12,000 a year in the U.S. plus $3,000 on a trip abroad.

- Active Cash: $240 + $60 rewards − $90 fees = **$210**
- Quicksilver: $180 + $45 = **$225**

Drop the trip to $1,500 and Active Cash wins ($240 + $30 − $45 = $225 vs $180 + $22.50 = $202.50).

Remember that "foreign" isn't only travel. Online orders from merchants that process payments outside the U.S. can trigger the fee too, even when you're shopping from home.

## Capital One Travel bookings

Quicksilver also lists **5% cash back on hotels, vacation rentals and rental car bookings made through Capital One Travel**. If you'd book that way anyway, it adds to Quicksilver's case for travelers. If you prefer booking direct with hotels or rental companies, ignore it and compare the flat rates.

## Who should pick which

**Pick Wells Fargo Active Cash if:**

- Almost everything you buy is in U.S. dollars
- You want the highest simple flat rate without an annual fee
- You already carry a no-foreign-fee card for trips

**Pick Capital One Quicksilver if:**

- You travel internationally or buy from overseas merchants regularly
- You want one card you never have to switch out at the border
- You'd use Capital One Travel for hotels or rental cars

## Using both

The cards don't compete if you split the jobs: put domestic spending on Active Cash for 2%, and keep Quicksilver for anything charged in a foreign currency. Both have a $0 annual fee, so keeping the second card costs nothing beyond the effort of remembering which one to use.

## Before you apply

- Confirm the current rate, fees and any welcome offer on the issuer's page — they can change.
- Check how each issuer pays out rewards and whether there's a minimum to redeem.
- If you pay interest, the rewards gap matters far less than the APR you're charged.
