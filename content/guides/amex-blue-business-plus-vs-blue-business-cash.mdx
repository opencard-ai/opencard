# Amex Blue Business Plus vs Blue Business Cash

American Express has two no-annual-fee Blue cards for businesses that look alike until you check how rewards pay out:

- [Blue Business Plus](/en/cards/amex-blue-biz-plus) — **Membership Rewards points**
- [Blue Business Cash](/en/cards/amex-blue-biz-cash) — **cash back** as a statement credit

Both earn at a **2x / 2%** headline rate on the first **$50,000** of purchases in a calendar year, then **1x / 1%** after that. The real choice is points you can transfer versus cash that needs no strategy.

Facts below come from American Express's card pages and our card records (checked October 26, 2026). Welcome offers change often and aren't quoted here.

## Side by side

| | Blue Business Plus | Blue Business Cash |
|---|---|---|
| Annual fee | $0 | $0 |
| Everyday rate | 2x Membership Rewards on the first $50,000 / year, then 1x | 2% cash back on the first $50,000 / year, then 1% |
| Cap | Calendar year | Calendar year |
| Rewards type | Transferable Membership Rewards points | Statement credit |
| Foreign transaction fee | Our record lists 2.7% | 2.7% |
| Best for | Businesses that already use (or want) Amex points | Businesses that want automatic cash |

On the first $50,000, the raw earn rate matches. After that, both drop to 1%. Hitting the cap mid-year is common for growing businesses — plan a second card for spend above $50,000, such as [Chase Ink Business Unlimited](/en/cards/chase-ink-biz-unlimited) at a flat 1.5% with no cap.

## What Plus actually buys you

Blue Business Plus is one of the cheapest ways into **Membership Rewards**. Points can transfer to airline and hotel partners, or book travel through Amex. That flexibility is the whole case for Plus over Cash.

It is **not** a high-earning category card. There is no grocery, ads or shipping bonus. If most of your spend is office supplies and gas, [Ink Business Cash](/en/cards/chase-ink-biz-cash) may earn more in those categories even though it doesn't earn Amex points.

Our [points valuation guide](/en/guides/credit-card-points-valuation-guide) explains why "2x points" is only worth more than 2% cash when you redeem thoughtfully.

## What Cash actually buys you

Blue Business Cash pays **2% cash back** on the first $50,000 each calendar year, credited automatically. No partners, no transfer bonuses, no portal. For a business that just wants a lower effective cost of spend, that simplicity wins.

The same **$50,000 calendar-year cap** applies. Seasonal businesses that front-load Q4 can burn the 2% rate early and sit on 1% until January 1.

## Break-even thinking (without inventing redemption values)

On the first $50,000:

- Cash gives you **$1,000** in statement credits (2%).
- Plus gives you **100,000** Membership Rewards points (2x).

If you redeem those points at about 1 cent each (a common floor through Amex travel or statement credit), Plus and Cash are roughly even. If you regularly transfer to partners at a higher effective value, Plus pulls ahead. If you never transfer and always take statement credits, Cash is less work for the same result.

Above $50,000, both earn 1%, so neither is a great catch-all for very high spend. Pair with a no-cap card.

## Foreign fees and other gotchas

Our records list a **2.7% foreign transaction fee** on Blue Business Cash, and the same figure for Blue Business Plus. Neither is a good overseas card. Keep supplier payments abroad on a no-FX card such as [BofA Business Advantage Travel Rewards](/en/cards/boa-biz-advantage-travel).

Both cards can add employee cards. Rewards usually pool to the primary account — confirm on Amex's page for the version you hold.

## Who should pick which

**Choose Blue Business Plus if:**
- You want Membership Rewards without an annual fee
- You already redeem Amex points (or are willing to learn)
- You spend under about $50,000 a year on this card, or have a plan for the rest

**Choose Blue Business Cash if:**
- You want automatic cash back and no points strategy
- Nobody on the team will transfer points
- You like Amex's billing tools but not the points ecosystem

**Consider neither as your only card if:**
- You need 5% categories — look at Ink Business Cash
- You want a flat uncapped rate — look at Ink Business Unlimited
- You need lounge access or travel credits — those live on premium cards with fees

For the cash Amex vs Chase flat-rate matchup, see [Blue Business Cash vs. Ink Business Unlimited](/en/guides/amex-blue-business-cash-vs-chase-ink-business-unlimited).


## Year-one examples (rates only)

**Business A — $30,000** on the card, all under the cap:

| Card | Rewards |
|---|---|
| Plus | 60,000 Membership Rewards points |
| Cash | $600 statement credit |

**Business B — $80,000** on the card:

| Card | Rewards |
|---|---|
| Plus | 100,000 points on the first $50,000 + 30,000 points on the next $30,000 = **130,000 points** |
| Cash | $1,000 on the first $50,000 + $300 on the next $30,000 = **$1,300** |

Business B should strongly consider moving spend above $50,000 to a no-cap card. Leaving $30,000 at 1% leaves money on the table versus a flat 1.5% product.

## How this pairs with Ink Business Unlimited

A practical two-card setup for many LLCs:

1. Blue Business Plus **or** Blue Business Cash for the first ~$50,000 (points or 2% cash)
2. [Ink Business Unlimited](/en/cards/chase-ink-biz-unlimited) for everything after that at 1.5% with no cap

You do not need both Blue cards. Pick Plus **or** Cash, then add a no-cap card if spend is high. Our [Blue Business Cash vs. Ink Business Unlimited](/en/guides/amex-blue-business-cash-vs-chase-ink-business-unlimited) guide walks through that second decision in detail.

## Credits, protections and what we are not claiming

Neither Blue card is a lounge card. Do not expect Centurion Lounge or Priority Pass access. Purchase protection and extended warranty appear on Amex's benefits materials for these products; rental car coverage on Blue Business Cash is secondary and excludes some countries. Read the Guide to Benefits before you decline the rental counter's coverage. More in our [rental car coverage guide](/en/guides/credit-card-rental-car-insurance-guide).

We are not quoting welcome-offer dollar amounts here because Amex rotates them. Blue Business Cash's page has shown an "as high as" structure; Plus has shown Membership Rewards bonuses after a minimum spend. Check the offer on your application screen.


## Employee cards and who should be the primary

Both cards allow employee cards at no extra card fee. Charges and rewards generally roll up to the business. Put the primary on whoever will actually redeem rewards — especially for Plus, where unused points help nobody. For Cash, redemption is automatic, so the bookkeeping owner matters more than the points hobbyist.

If contractors should not see the full credit line, Amex's employee-card controls are worth reading before you hand out plastic. That is an operations choice, not a rewards choice, but it trips up first-time business-card applicants.

## Quick checklist

1. Decide points vs cash before you compare welcome offers.
2. Estimate whether this card will clear $50,000 in a calendar year.
3. Keep overseas spend off both cards if a 2.7% FX fee applies.
4. Pay in full each month; interest erases a 2% rate quickly.
5. Confirm the current welcome offer and terms on American Express's site before applying.
